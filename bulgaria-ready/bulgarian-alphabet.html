The six letters that trip everyone up

These look like Latin letters you already know. They are not. Getting these six wrong is the difference between reading a sign and guessing at it.

  • В в is V, not B. Вход = "vhod" = entrance.
  • Н н is N, not H. Не = "ne" = no.
  • Р р is a rolled R, not P. Ресторант = "restorant".
  • С с is S, not C. Сметка = "smetka" = the bill.
  • У у is OO, not Y. Улица = "ulitsa" = street.
  • Х х is a throaty H, not X. Хляб = "hlyab" = bread.

And one that has no English equivalent at all. The letter Ъ is a vowel — a short "uh" sound, like the u in "curt". It is never silent and never a hard sign, whatever you may have learned about Russian. You'll meet it immediately: България is "Bul-ga-ria".

Common questions

About the Bulgarian alphabet

How many letters are in the Bulgarian alphabet?

Thirty. Six are vowels (а, е, и, о, у, ъ) and the rest are consonants, plus ь which modifies the consonant before it rather than making a sound of its own.

Is Bulgarian the same as Russian?

No. Both use Cyrillic, but the alphabets differ and the languages are not mutually intelligible. Russian has 33 letters to Bulgarian's 30. The biggest trap for anyone coming from Russian is Ъ, which is a full vowel in Bulgarian but a silent hard sign in Russian.

Where did the Cyrillic alphabet come from?

It was developed in the First Bulgarian Empire in the 9th century, by students of Saints Cyril and Methodius, and spread from there across the Slavic world. Bulgaria marks it every year on 24 May, which is a public holiday.

How long does it take to learn to read Bulgarian?

Reading — sounding words out — takes most people a few hours, because Bulgarian spelling is close to phonetic. Understanding what you've read is a different and much longer job, but being able to decode a street sign, a bill or a pharmacy window is genuinely quick.

Do I need Bulgarian to live in Bulgaria?

You can get by in tourist areas and among younger people in cities without it. Away from the coast and in any dealing with a bank, a notary, a council office or a builder, it stops being optional very quickly — and reading the alphabet is the first step that pays for itself.
