Basic Bulgarian phrases

About sixty words and phrases, grouped by when you'll need them. Not a language course — the specific things that get you through a first week, a first shop and a first appointment.

Can't read the Cyrillic yet? Start with the alphabet guide — it takes an afternoon.

Before anything else: the nod means no

In Bulgaria, shaking your head side to side means yes, and nodding up and down means no. It is the exact opposite of what your body does automatically, and it causes genuine confusion in shops, at viewings and in negotiations. Bulgarians dealing with foreigners often switch to the Western version to be helpful, which makes it worse — you can never be sure which system you're in. When it matters, say the word: да (da) for yes, не (ne) for no.

The emergency number is 112

Same across the EU, and it works in Bulgaria from any phone. Operators in cities generally speak English; in rural areas assume they may not, and have your address written down in Cyrillic somewhere you can reach it.

Three things about saying it

Stress matters more than sounds. Bulgarian stress moves around and isn't marked in writing. The capitals in the pronunciation column above show where to lean. Getting the stress wrong is the main reason a correctly pronounced word doesn't land.

There's no "the" as a separate word. It gets stuck on the end instead: сметка is "a bill", сметката is "the bill". That's why you ask for сметката, моля and not сметка, моля.

Formal and informal are different words. Здравейте and Здравей both mean hello, but using the informal one on a notary or an official lands badly. When in doubt, use the longer one — it ends in -те.
